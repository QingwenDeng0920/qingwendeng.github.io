We argue that administrative reforms can operate as institutional purges in authoritarian states, conceptualizing administrative units as institutionalized platforms through which authoritarian rulers anchor and distribute political resources. We develop a formal model in which the ruler chooses among retention, personnel purge, and institutional purge, predicting that centrally embedded units are less likely to be erased while local factionalism raises purge risk mainly when local organization is weakly tied to central elite networks. We test these implications with an original dataset linking Vietnam's 2025 province-level consolidation, 304 central elite profiles, and 1,031 local elite career-network nodes to the selective erasure of institutional continuity, developing institutional purge as a theory of authoritarian power sharing.
